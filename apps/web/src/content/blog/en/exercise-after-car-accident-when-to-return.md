Before the crash you ran three mornings a week, lifted after work or played soccer on Saturdays. Now your neck is stiff, your back complains when you bend and you do not know whether going back to the gym will help or make everything worse.

Both fears are reasonable. **For most strain and sprain injuries after a crash, movement is part of the treatment, not the enemy: the current advice is to stay as active as you comfortably can instead of resting in bed. But getting back to hard training is a gradual process, and some injuries, such as a concussion, broken ribs or a nerve problem, need clear limits and a provider's go-ahead first.**

## Why moving usually helps

After a crash, the most common injuries are strains and sprains of the muscles and ligaments of the neck and back. For acute low back pain, MedlinePlus advises against bed rest and recommends staying as active as possible, cutting back activity only for the first couple of days. For neck pain after a traffic collision, the OPTIMa guideline recommends gentle range-of-motion exercise as part of early care.

The reason is simple: a sore muscle that is not used gets stiffer and weaker, and fear of moving keeps that cycle going. Read more in [passive vs active care](/en/blog/passive-vs-active-care-after-car-accident) and [the first six weeks of physical therapy after a crash](/en/blog/first-six-weeks-physical-therapy-after-crash).

But "stay active" is not the same as "go back to your old workout tomorrow." Walking, gentle mobility and light everyday activity come first. Heavy lifting, running, contact sports and high-intensity classes come later.

## Before you start: get the injury checked

A few injuries change the plan completely, so it is worth an evaluation before you train hard:

- **Concussion.** The CDC recommends a gradual return to activity and avoiding anything that could cause another blow to the head (contact sports, sparring, activities with a risk of falling) until a provider says it is safe. Read [concussion after a car accident](/en/blog/concussion-after-car-accident).
- **Broken or bruised ribs.** The NHS notes they usually heal in about 3 to 6 weeks and advises against strenuous exercise and heavy lifting while they heal. See [chest and rib pain from the seatbelt](/en/blog/chest-rib-pain-seatbelt-after-car-accident).
- **Disc or nerve injury.** Pain that runs down an arm or a leg, pins and needles or weakness need a provider's plan before loaded exercises like squats and deadlifts. See [herniated disc and sciatica](/en/blog/herniated-disc-sciatica-after-car-accident).
- **Fractures and surgery.** Return depends on the bone and the procedure; follow your surgeon's timeline, not a general article.

## A gradual return, step by step

There is no single timeline that fits everyone, but this is the logic most rehab programs follow. Move to the next stage only when the current one feels manageable the same day and the next morning.

A few practical rules help at every stage:

- **Increase one thing at a time:** weight, distance or speed, not all three in the same week.
- **Mild soreness that eases is expected; sharp pain is not.** Stop if pain is sharp, shoots into an arm or leg, or is clearly worse the next morning, and drop back a stage.
- **Neck injuries:** avoid at first exercises that load or jerk the neck, like heavy shrugs, overhead presses, burpees and headers in soccer.
- **Back injuries:** MedlinePlus advises avoiding heavy lifting and twisting of the back during the first weeks after acute low back pain begins.
- **Watch the medicines.** Some muscle relaxers and pain medicines cause drowsiness; do not train with heavy weights or run near traffic if you feel drowsy or dizzy. See [ibuprofen or Tylenol after a car accident](/en/blog/pain-relievers-after-car-accident).

## Exercise and your claim

Two things to keep in mind if you have an injury claim. First, tell your provider what you are doing in the gym and how it feels, so it is in your record: gradual return to activity is a normal part of recovery. Second, insurers can look at public social media; a photo at the gym without context can be used to argue you were not hurt. Keep your treatment consistent too, because [gaps in treatment can hurt your claim](/en/blog/gaps-in-treatment-hurt-your-claim).

## The short version

After most crash injuries, gentle movement helps recovery, and bed rest does not. Start with walking and simple mobility, add light cardio and light strength, and increase gradually, using how you feel the next day as your guide. Get evaluated first, and wait for clearance, if you had a concussion, rib injury, fracture or nerve symptoms. Your long-term goal is the same as for everyone: regular activity most days of the week. For a sense of normal timelines, read [how long car accident recovery takes](/en/blog/how-long-car-accident-recovery-takes).
