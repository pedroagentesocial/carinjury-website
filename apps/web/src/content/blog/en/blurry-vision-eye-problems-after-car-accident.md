In a crash, the eyes take more than people expect. The airbag hits the face, glass breaks, the head snaps forward and back, and sometimes the face meets the steering wheel or the window. Afterward you may notice a bright red patch on the white of your eye, a bruise around it, or something less visible: blurry vision, seeing double, or new flashes and floaters.

Some of these are harmless and some are emergencies. **Vision that changes, double vision, a painful red eye or a sudden shower of floaters or flashes need to be checked the same day, in the ER or by an eye doctor.** A painless red spot with normal vision is usually not dangerous.

## The red spot on the white of the eye

MedlinePlus describes a subconjunctival hemorrhage as a bright red patch on the white of the eye from a small blood vessel bleeding under the clear surface. It can follow an injury, but also violent coughing or sneezing, rubbing the eye, high blood pressure or blood thinners. **It does not hurt and it does not change your vision**, and it usually goes away on its own in about 2 to 3 weeks. MedlinePlus still advises contacting a provider when one appears. If the eye is also painful or your vision is not normal, it is not "just" a red spot.

## A blow to the eye or the face

MedlinePlus explains that blunt trauma can cause a black eye from bleeding under the skin and can also cause **hyphema**, blood inside the front of the eye. Its first aid for a blow, cut or scratch: put a clean, cool compress on the eye **without pressing**, and get medical help right away. If blood is pooling in the eye, cover both eyes.

Car and motorcycle crashes are among the common causes of facial injuries, and MedlinePlus lists double vision and swelling or bruising around the eyes that can cause vision problems among their symptoms. On exam, a provider looks for changes in vision or in how the eyes move, and may order X-rays or a CT of the face. A broken jaw or teeth can come with these injuries too; see [jaw pain after a car accident](/en/blog/jaw-pain-tmj-after-car-accident).

## Flashes, floaters or a shadow: the retina

The National Eye Institute (NEI) lists eye injury among the most common causes of retinal detachment, together with aging. The warning signs are a **sudden increase in floaters** (small dark spots or squiggly lines), **flashes of light** and **a shadow over part of your vision**. NEI calls retinal detachment a medical emergency: see your eye doctor or go to the ER right away, because delay lets more of the retina detach and raises the risk of permanent vision loss. Treated in time, NEI says treatment works for about 9 out of 10 people.

## Airbag dust, glass and chemicals

Airbag powder and broken glass can get into the eyes. For a small particle, MedlinePlus advises letting blinking and tears wash it out, then flushing gently with clean water if it stays, and getting help if discomfort or blurry vision continues. For a chemical, flush with cool tap water for 15 minutes with the injured eye tilted down and to the side, and get help right away. **If something is stuck in the eye, leave it there**, do not press, and get emergency care. More in [airbag injuries after a car accident](/en/blog/airbag-injuries-after-car-accident) and [cuts and broken glass after a car accident](/en/blog/cuts-broken-glass-after-car-accident).

## When the eyes are fine but the vision is not: concussion

Vision problems are one of the physical symptoms of a concussion, according to the CDC, and "one pupil larger than the other or double vision" is on its list of danger signs that need 911 or the emergency department. A concussion can happen without hitting your head. If blurry vision comes with headache, dizziness, nausea or trouble concentrating, read [concussion after a car accident](/en/blog/concussion-after-car-accident), [dizziness or vertigo after a car accident](/en/blog/dizziness-vertigo-after-car-accident) and [headaches after a car accident](/en/blog/headaches-after-car-accident). And do not drive while your vision is not right.

## The short version

After a crash, a painless red patch on the eye with normal vision usually clears in 2 to 3 weeks. Blurry or double vision, a painful red eye, blood inside the eye, something stuck in it, or new flashes, floaters or a shadow need care the same day, because some of these can cost you vision if they wait. And vision changes without an obvious eye injury may be a concussion. Keep a record of every symptom and visit; see [how to document your injuries for an insurance claim](/en/blog/how-to-document-injuries-insurance-claim).
