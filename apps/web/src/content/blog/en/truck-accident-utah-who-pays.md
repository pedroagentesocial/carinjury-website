You were driving on I-15 or Bangerter when a semi changed lanes into you, or a delivery truck rear-ended you at a light. The damage to your car looks terrible, and the truck barely has a scratch.

A crash with a large truck is not just a bigger car accident. **The physics are different, the rules the truck has to follow are different and the insurance is usually handled by a company that moves fast.** Here is what changes and what does not.

## Why truck crashes injure more

The Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) puts it simply: the heaviest trucks can weigh 20 to 30 times as much as a passenger car, and a loaded tractor-trailer needs **20% to 40% more distance to stop** than a car. In 2024, 5,340 people died in large-truck crashes in the U.S., and 62% of them were in cars and other passenger vehicles.

Most people who survive a truck crash have the same kinds of injuries we see in other crashes — whiplash, back injuries, concussions, shoulder and knee injuries — but often more severe, and more often several at once. See [common car accident injuries and their symptoms](/en/blog/common-car-accident-injuries-symptoms).

## The rules the truck has to follow

Commercial trucks that operate under federal rules have obligations ordinary drivers do not. A few that often matter after a crash:

    </thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>11-hour driving limit</td><td>A property-carrying driver may drive up to 11 hours after 10 consecutive hours off duty</td><td>Fatigue: the logs show how long the driver had been behind the wheel</td></tr>
      <tr><td>14-hour window</td><td>No driving after the 14th consecutive hour after coming on duty</td><td>Long shifts can be checked against the time of the crash</td></tr>
      <tr><td>30-minute break</td><td>Required after 8 hours of driving without a 30-minute interruption</td><td>Another point to compare with the driver's records</td></tr>
      <tr><td>60/70-hour limit</td><td>No driving after 60 hours on duty in 7 days, or 70 hours in 8 days, depending on the carrier's schedule</td><td>Shows the driver's workload over the whole week</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Markings on the truck</td><td>The carrier's name and USDOT number on both sides of the vehicle</td><td>Identifies the company responsible for the truck</td></tr>

There are exceptions to these limits (for example, for adverse weather and some short-haul drivers), so a lawyer has to check which ones apply. But the point is that **a truck crash leaves a paper and electronic trail that a normal crash does not.**

## Evidence that can disappear

Federal rules require carriers to keep drivers' records of duty status and supporting documents for **at least six months**. After that, they may be gone. Dash cameras, electronic logs, dispatch messages, maintenance records and the truck's own data can also be overwritten or discarded.

That is why truck cases are one of the situations where it makes sense to talk to an attorney early. A lawyer can ask the company in writing to preserve that evidence before it is lost. Read [Utah car accident deadlines](/en/blog/utah-car-accident-claim-deadlines) for the legal windows, which in some cases are much shorter than four years.

## Who pays your medical bills

Utah is a no-fault state, and that does not change because the other vehicle was a truck: **your own PIP pays your first medical bills**, regardless of who caused the crash. Read [who pays my medical bills after a car accident in Utah](/en/blog/who-pays-medical-bills-car-accident-utah).

The difference comes later, in the claim against whoever caused the crash. For an interstate for-hire carrier hauling general freight, federal rules require at least **$750,000** in liability coverage, far above the minimum for a private car. Depending on the facts, more than one party may share responsibility: the driver, the trucking company and sometimes others, such as whoever loaded the cargo or maintained the truck. That also means more insurers and more lawyers on the other side.

## The short version

A crash with a semi or commercial truck usually causes more serious injuries and creates more evidence. Get evaluated the same day, photograph the company name and USDOT number, do not give statements to the trucking insurer and talk to an attorney early, before logs and recordings are discarded. Your PIP still pays first; the claim against the truck comes later.
