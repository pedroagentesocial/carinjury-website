Your neck gets most of the attention after a crash, but some people notice something different: a sharp or aching pain right at the very bottom of the spine. It is worst when you sit, the drive to work suddenly feels long, and it bites when you stand up from a chair.

That is the tailbone, or coccyx: a small bone made of three to five fused vertebrae at the tip of the spine. **Most tailbone injuries are bruises that heal in about a month, and most people get relief with home care. But a few warning signs mean the problem may be higher up, in the spine or the nerves, and need care right away.**

## How the tailbone gets hurt

MedlinePlus says the most common cause of tailbone trauma is a backward fall onto a hard surface, like ice or a slippery floor. A crash is not a fall, but it can do something similar: the body is thrown down and back into the seat with force. Cleveland Clinic notes that a bad fall can bruise, break or dislocate the tailbone, and that the pain may show up as a dull ache or a sharp, piercing pain.

Fractures are actually uncommon. MedlinePlus explains that most tailbone injuries are bruising and pain; only in rare cases is the bone broken.

## What it feels like

- **Pain when sitting** or putting pressure on the tailbone.
- **Pain when you go from sitting to standing.**
- **Pain during bowel movements or sex.**
- **Bruising** over the lower part of the spine.

Tailbone pain can travel with other problems from the same crash: low back pain, pain down the leg or trouble sleeping. If the pain spreads down a leg, read [herniated disc and sciatica after a car accident](/en/blog/herniated-disc-sciatica-after-car-accident); for the rest of the back, [back pain after a car accident](/en/blog/back-pain-after-car-accident) and [hip pain after a car accident](/en/blog/hip-pain-after-car-accident).

## Home care that helps

These steps come from MedlinePlus's aftercare instructions for tailbone injuries:

- **Take the pressure off.** Sit on a cushion or gel donut; the hole in the center reduces pressure on the tailbone. Avoid sitting for long periods and do not sit on hard surfaces. When you do sit, shift your weight from one buttock to the other.
- **Ice it.** About 20 minutes every hour while you are awake for the first 48 hours, then 2 to 3 times a day. Never put ice directly on the skin.
- **Sleep on your belly** to take pressure off the tailbone.
- **Pain relievers.** Ibuprofen, naproxen or acetaminophen can help. Talk with your provider first if you have heart disease, high blood pressure, kidney or liver disease, or have had stomach ulcers or internal bleeding, and do not take more than the label says. Read [ibuprofen or Tylenol after a car accident](/en/blog/pain-relievers-after-car-accident).
- **Prevent constipation.** Painful bowel movements are common with this injury. Eat plenty of fiber, drink plenty of fluids and use a stool softener if needed.
- **Move, gently.** Once the pain eases, start with light activity like walking and increase it slowly. Ice afterward if it is sore.

## Tell your provider, even if it feels embarrassing

Tailbone pain is easy to leave out of the conversation, especially when it involves sitting or the bathroom. Say it anyway, on your first visit. If it is not in your chart from the start, it is harder to connect it to the crash later. And pain that changes how you sit can affect your work, your driving and your sleep; see [trouble sleeping after a car accident](/en/blog/trouble-sleeping-after-car-accident) and [how to document your injuries for an insurance claim](/en/blog/how-to-document-injuries-insurance-claim).

## The short version

Pain at the very bottom of the spine that flares when you sit or stand up after a crash is usually a bruised tailbone, which heals in about 4 weeks; a fracture is less common and takes 8 to 12 weeks. A cushion, ice, less sitting and avoiding constipation help most people. Leg numbness or weakness, or bladder or bowel problems, need care right away, and pain that does not improve deserves an evaluation, because there are effective options beyond waiting.
