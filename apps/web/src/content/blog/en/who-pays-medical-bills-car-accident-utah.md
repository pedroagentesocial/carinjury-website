It is the first question almost every patient asks us in Taylorsville: *"If the other driver caused the crash, why is the bill coming to me?"*

The answer is that Utah is a **no-fault state**. Fault decides who pays *in the end* — it does not decide who pays *first*.

## What "no-fault" really means in Utah

Utah law requires every auto policy to include **Personal Injury Protection (PIP)**. PIP is your own insurance, and it pays your first medical bills **regardless of who caused the crash** — even if you were rear-ended at a red light and did nothing wrong.

That is good news and bad news at the same time:

<div class="pa-compare">
  <div class="pa-col pa-col-good">
    <h4>What PIP pays for</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Medical care: exams, X-rays, chiropractic, physical therapy, rehab</li>
      <li>Ambulance and hospital charges</li>
      <li>Prescriptions and medical supplies</li>
      <li>Lost income: 85% of gross pay, capped at $250/week for up to 52 weeks</li>
      <li>Household services you can no longer do: up to $20/day for 365 days</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-col pa-col-bad">
    <h4>What PIP does NOT pay for</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Pain and suffering</li>
      <li>Damage to your vehicle (that is a separate property claim)</li>
      <li>Anything above your policy limit — often just $3,000</li>
      <li>Wage loss above the $250/week cap</li>
      <li>Bills you never submitted, or care that was never documented</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

## The order in which your bills actually get paid

<div class="pa-timeline">
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Step 1 · Day 0</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Your own PIP</p>
    <p>You open a PIP claim with <strong>your</strong> insurer and get a claim number. Every provider bills that number. This happens before anyone decides who was at fault.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Step 2 · When PIP is exhausted</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Health insurance, MedPay or a lien</p>
    <p>Once the $3,000 is used up, your health insurance, optional MedPay coverage, or an agreement with the clinic covers continuing care. Treatment should not stop here.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Step 3 · At the end of the case</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">The at-fault driver's liability insurance</p>
    <p>The at-fault driver's insurer reimburses everything else — remaining bills, pain and suffering, wage loss beyond the cap — in a single settlement, months later.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="pa-note">
  <span class="pa-note-icon">⚖️</span>
  <div>
    <p><strong>The $3,000 threshold cuts both ways.</strong> Under Utah Code § 31A-22-309, you can only bring a claim against the at-fault driver for pain and suffering once your reasonable medical expenses exceed <strong>$3,000</strong> — or if you suffered permanent disability, permanent disfigurement, dismemberment or death.</p>
    <p>In other words: the same number that limits your PIP is the door you have to walk through to claim anything more. Under-treating an injury does not just hurt your neck — it can close that door.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Why $3,000 disappears faster than people expect

Three thousand dollars sounds like a lot until you price out a single emergency-room visit with imaging. One ER trip with a CT scan can consume the entire minimum PIP benefit before you have had your first follow-up appointment.

That is why the *sequence* matters so much. Patients who wait a month "to see if it gets better" usually burn their PIP on emergency care and have nothing left for the rehabilitation that actually restores function. If that describes you, read [what happens in the first 24 hours after a crash](/en/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-after-accident).

<div class="pa-check">
  <h4>What to do in your first week</h4>
  <ul>
    <li>Call <strong>your own</strong> insurer and open a PIP claim — not the other driver's.</li>
    <li>Write down the PIP claim number and give it to every provider you see.</li>
    <li>Get evaluated even if you feel "mostly fine". Documentation started late is documentation that is easy to attack.</li>
    <li>Ask your clinic whether they bill PIP directly (we do) so you are not paying out of pocket up front.</li>
    <li>Keep receipts for mileage, medications and household help — those are reimbursable too.</li>
    <li>Do not accept a quick settlement before you know the full extent of the injury.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

## The short version

Fault does not determine who pays your first bills — **your own PIP does**. The legal minimum is $3,000, it runs out quickly, and that same $3,000 is also the threshold that unlocks a claim against the at-fault driver. Getting evaluated early and documented consistently protects both your recovery and your case.

If you are unsure what your policy covers, bring your declarations page to your first visit. We read it with you and bill PIP directly, so you know exactly where you stand before treatment starts.
