In the second before the crash you gripped the wheel or threw your hands against the dashboard. Afterward your wrist was sore, but you could move it, so you figured it was a sprain. Two weeks later it still hurts when you turn a key or push yourself up from a chair.

Most wrist pain after a crash is a sprain or a bruise. But **some fractures look and feel like a sprain at first, and one of them, the scaphoid fracture, can be invisible on the first X-ray.** Here is what to watch for.

## How a crash injures the wrist and hand

- **Bracing.** Locking your arms against the wheel or the dashboard sends the force of the impact straight into your wrists, much like falling onto an outstretched hand, which is the classic way wrists break.
- **The airbag.** Hands on the wheel can be thrown back when the bag inflates. See [airbag injuries after a car accident](/en/blog/airbag-injuries-after-car-accident).
- **A direct blow** from the door, the window or something loose in the car.

The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS) notes that a car crash can break the wrist of a young, healthy person. Healthy bones are not a guarantee.

## Sprain or fracture?

AAOS says a hidden ("occult") fracture can be mistaken for a mild or moderate sprain, and the most common example is the scaphoid. Wrist sprains are graded from 1 (ligaments stretched) to 3 (ligament completely torn or pulled off the bone).

## Why the scaphoid gets its own warning

The scaphoid is small, and its blood supply flows "backwards", from the end closest to the fingers. That is why fractures near the other end heal more slowly, and why a missed scaphoid fracture can fail to heal (nonunion) or lose its blood supply (avascular necrosis). Over time, both can lead to arthritis in the wrist.

That is also why doctors do not just wait. If a scaphoid fracture is suspected but the X-ray looks normal, AAOS describes wearing a splint or cast for 2 to 3 weeks and then repeating the X-ray. An MRI can sometimes show the fracture sooner, and a CT shows whether the pieces have moved. Learn what each test shows in [X-ray, CT or MRI after a car accident](/en/blog/xray-mri-ct-after-car-accident).

## What recovery looks like

<div class="pa-timeline">
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">First days</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Protect it and get it checked</p>
    <p>For a suspected sprain, ice for 20 minutes at a time, several times a day, never directly on the skin, and do not wrap a compression bandage too tight. If pain and swelling last more than 48 hours, or pinching and gripping hurt at the base of the thumb, get evaluated.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Weeks 4–6</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Out of the cast</p>
    <p>When a radius fracture does not need surgery, the cast usually comes off about 4 to 6 weeks after the injury. Almost everyone has some stiffness at first, and it usually improves over the next month or two.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Around 3 months</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Back to most activities</p>
    <p>Most distal radius fractures heal enough for all activities in about 3 months; full recovery can take up to a year. Scaphoid fractures can need a cast for longer, sometimes up to 6 months, and smoking can delay or prevent healing.</p>
  </div>
</div>

Mild sprains often recover fully over several weeks. Ligaments repaired with surgery usually heal in 8 to 12 weeks, though full recovery can take 6 to 12 months.

## Numbness, tingling and the rest of the arm

Tingling in the fingers after a crash does not always start in the wrist. It can come from the neck, the shoulder or the elbow, and the treatment depends on where. Read [numbness or tingling in your arms or hands](/en/blog/numbness-tingling-arms-hands-after-car-accident) and [shoulder pain after a car accident](/en/blog/shoulder-pain-after-car-accident).

## The short version

Bracing against the wheel or the dashboard and the airbag itself can sprain or break the wrist. Most pain is a sprain, but a scaphoid fracture can feel like one and can be missed on the first X-ray, which is why a sore thumb side of the wrist deserves a splint and a second look. Numb fingers or a wrist that looks bent need the ER right away. And if pain and swelling are still there after 48 hours, get it checked rather than waiting it out.
