You were crossing the street, walking through a parking lot or waiting at the corner, and a car hit you. There is no "other car" against yours: there is just you, your body and the driver's vehicle.

That raises a very common question: **"If I was not driving, which insurance pays?"** In Utah, the answer is better than many people think. But first, your health.

## What to do at the scene

<div class="pa-check">
  <h4>If you can do it safely</h4>
  <ul>
    <li>Get out of the traffic lane and call 911.</li>
    <li>Ask for the driver's name, phone number, license, plate number and insurance information.</li>
    <li>Take photos of the vehicle, the place, the crosswalk, traffic lights, signs and your injuries.</li>
    <li>Ask witnesses for their names and phone numbers.</li>
    <li>Look around for security cameras at businesses or homes nearby.</li>
    <li>Do not argue about who was at fault or accept money at the scene.</li>
    <li>Ask the police officer how to get a copy of the report.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

Even if you feel fine, get a medical evaluation the same day or as soon as possible. Many symptoms appear hours or days later: see [what to do in the first 24 hours after an accident](/en/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-after-accident).

## Who pays your medical bills

Utah is a no-fault state: auto policies must include **Personal Injury Protection (PIP)**, which pays the first medical bills regardless of who caused the crash. And PIP is not only for the people inside the car.

The Utah PIP minimum is **$3,000** and runs out fast. After that, care is usually paid by health insurance, MedPay or an agreement with the clinic, and later by a claim against the driver who caused the crash. Read the details in [who pays medical bills after a car accident in Utah](/en/blog/who-pays-medical-bills-car-accident-utah).

## What if they say it was your fault?

Utah law requires drivers to yield to pedestrians in a crosswalk. But even if you were crossing outside the crosswalk or against the light, you may still be able to recover damages. Utah uses a modified comparative fault rule: you can recover as long as your share of fault is less than the driver's, reduced by your percentage. See [what happens if you were partly at fault](/en/blog/partly-at-fault-car-accident-utah).

The driver's insurer may try to blame you. Do not give a recorded statement or sign anything before you understand your rights: see [what to say to the insurance adjuster](/en/blog/what-to-say-insurance-adjuster).

## Common injuries in pedestrians

The bumper usually hits the legs first, then the body hits the hood or the windshield, and then the ground. That is why it is common to see knee and leg injuries, hip and pelvis injuries, broken wrists from breaking the fall, and head injuries. Read more about [concussion after a car accident](/en/blog/concussion-after-car-accident).

## The short version

If a car hit you while walking, call 911, document everything and get evaluated even if you feel fine. In Utah, the PIP of the vehicle that hit you, or your own household's policy, usually pays the first medical bills, even if you do not have a car. In a hit-and-run, report it to the police right away. And do not accept blame or sign anything at the scene.
