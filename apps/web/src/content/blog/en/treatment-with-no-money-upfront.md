The $3,000 of PIP is gone. You have no health insurance, or a deductible you cannot cover. The doctor says you need eight more weeks of rehabilitation.

This is the point where most people quietly stop treatment — and it is the single most expensive decision in a car accident claim, medically and financially.

There is a standard mechanism for exactly this situation, and it is worth understanding properly before anyone hands you a form.

## What a letter of protection actually is

A **letter of protection (LOP)** is a written agreement sent by your attorney to a medical provider. It says: treat this patient now, do not send them to collections, and you will be paid out of the settlement or verdict when the case resolves.

A **medical lien** is the related concept from the provider's side: a legal claim against the eventual settlement proceeds for the value of the care given.

<div class="pa-timeline">
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Step 1</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">The agreement is signed</p>
    <p>Your attorney sends the LOP to the clinic. You sign an acknowledgement. Nothing is paid at this point.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Step 2</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Treatment proceeds normally</p>
    <p>You attend appointments and receive care. The clinic bills your attorney's office rather than you, and does not send the account to collections while the case is open.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Step 3</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">The record builds</p>
    <p>Your bills and records accumulate. This documentation is also what proves your damages — the treatment and the evidence are the same thing.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Step 4</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">The case resolves and providers are paid</p>
    <p>From the settlement, your attorney pays the lien holders — usually after negotiating the balances down — and the remainder goes to you.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Be clear about the trade-off

<div class="pa-compare">
  <div class="pa-col pa-col-good">
    <h4>What it solves</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Care continues when PIP is exhausted and there is no health coverage</li>
      <li>No payment at time of service, no collections calls during the case</li>
      <li>Avoids the treatment gap that would otherwise damage the claim</li>
      <li>Keeps the medical record continuous and credible</li>
      <li>Lets your attorney negotiate the balances at the end</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-col pa-col-bad">
    <h4>What it does not do</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>Make treatment free — the debt exists from day one</li>
      <li>Guarantee the settlement covers it</li>
      <li>Protect you if the case is lost or settles for very little</li>
      <li>Come at insurance-negotiated rates automatically</li>
      <li>Replace using PIP or health insurance where those are available</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="pa-note pa-note-alert">
  <span class="pa-note-icon">⚠️</span>
  <div>
    <p><strong>The honest risk.</strong> A letter of protection is deferred payment funded by the outcome of your case. If the claim fails or settles for less than the total of the liens, you can remain responsible for the balance. Ask that question directly — in writing — before you sign, and ask whether the clinic reduces its balance when a settlement falls short.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="pa-check">
  <h4>Ask these before signing anything</h4>
  <ul>
    <li>Am I personally responsible if the case is lost or settles low?</li>
    <li>What are the rates, and are they the same as your cash or insured rates?</li>
    <li>Will you reduce the balance if the settlement does not cover it?</li>
    <li>Does the agreement charge interest?</li>
    <li>Will you keep the account out of collections while the case is open?</li>
    <li>Can I have a copy of everything I sign, today?</li>
    <li>Is my attorney reviewing this agreement before I sign it?</li>

## Why clinics agree to this at all

It is a reasonable question. The answer is that a car accident clinic that treats on liens is betting on documented, medically necessary care — the kind that survives review. That alignment is real, but it also means you should expect a clinic to hold you to the plan: attend appointments, follow the home program, and be discharged when you are actually better rather than when the calendar says so.

## The short version

A letter of protection is not free care; it is care now, paid from the case later. Used properly it prevents the treatment gap that would otherwise cost you far more than the bill. Used carelessly — without reading the terms or involving your attorney — it can leave you owing money at the end of a case you thought was over.
