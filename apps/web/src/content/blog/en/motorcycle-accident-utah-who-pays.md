A car turned left without seeing you. You were on the bike, helmet on, in your lane. Now your leg is scraped, your shoulder hurts and you have one question: **who pays the hospital?**

In a car crash, the answer in Utah almost always starts with PIP. On a motorcycle, not necessarily, and it is worth knowing before you need it.

## The big difference: PIP

Utah law (section 31A-22-302) says motorcycles, like trailers and some off-highway vehicles, **are not required to have PIP**. In a car, PIP pays your first medical bills no matter who was at fault. On a motorcycle, if your policy does not include it, that first safety net is not there.

That changes where the money comes from at first:

## The bias against riders

Many adjusters start from the assumption that the rider "was speeding" or "was weaving between cars." In Utah, your share of fault decides whether you can collect and how much: at 50% or more against the other driver, you do not collect from them. Read [how Utah's 50% rule works](/en/blog/partly-at-fault-car-accident-utah).

Evidence is what breaks the bias:

  <h4>What to do after a motorcycle accident</h4>

    <li>If you can, get out of the roadway, but do not take your helmet off if your neck or head hurts: wait for paramedics.</li>
    <li>Call the police and get the report number.</li>

    <li>Get evaluated the same day. Road rash gets infected, and head injuries can show symptoms late.</li>
    <li>Do not give recorded statements or sign anything with the insurer before talking to an attorney.</li>

## The most common motorcycle injuries

- **Road rash.** It looks superficial, but it can be deep, carry dirt or asphalt and get infected. It needs cleaning and medical follow-up.
- **Fractures** of the legs, arms, collarbone and wrists, from the impact or the fall.
- **Head injuries**, even with a helmet. Check the signs in [concussion after a car accident](/en/blog/concussion-after-car-accident).
- **Neck, back and shoulder injuries**, which often show up in the following days. Read about [delayed symptoms](/en/blog/delayed-symptoms-after-car-accident).

## The helmet: law and common sense

In Utah, **people under 21** must wear a helmet to operate or ride a motorcycle, under section 41-6a-1505. From 21 on, the law does not require it, but the CDC is clear: helmets reduce the risk of head injury. Your helmet and protective gear also help show you were riding carefully.

## The short version

In Utah, motorcycle insurance is not required to have PIP, so your first medical bills may come from your health insurance or from optional medical coverage. Check today whether your policy has medical coverage and UM/UIM. If you have already had the accident, document everything, keep the bike and gear as evidence, and talk to an attorney before accepting any percentage of fault.
