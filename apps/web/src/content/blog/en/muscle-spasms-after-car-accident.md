The day after the crash, you turn your head to check the mirror and your neck grabs. Or you bend to pick something up and your lower back locks so hard you have to hold on to the counter. Sometimes it is a hard knot at the top of the shoulder that does not let go for days.

That is a muscle spasm, and it is one of the most common things patients describe after a car accident. **Most spasms after a crash come from strained muscles and improve within days to weeks with movement and simple care. But a spasm is a symptom, not a diagnosis: it is the body reacting to an injury, and a few of those injuries need to be ruled out.**

## What a muscle spasm is

MedlinePlus describes a muscle cramp as a muscle that tightens on its own and does not relax; the muscle can feel hard or bulging. Among the causes it lists are **overusing or injuring a muscle** and dehydration. After a crash, a spasm is often the muscles around an injured area tightening hard, as if to protect it. That is why it feels like a lock: it limits the movement that hurts.

The problem is that a muscle held tight for a long time also gets sore and tired, and moving less makes it stiffer. That cycle, pain, tightening and less movement, is what good treatment tries to break.

## Where spasms show up after a crash

- **Neck and the top of the shoulders.** The classic sign of whiplash: the neck feels stiff, you cannot turn your head fully and the muscles between the neck and shoulder are hard to the touch. Read [what is whiplash](/en/blog/what-is-whiplash).
- **Lower back.** Bracing and twisting in the seat strain the muscles beside the spine. See [back pain after a car accident](/en/blog/back-pain-after-car-accident).
- **Between the shoulder blades.** Where the shoulder belt crosses and posture muscles work all day. See [upper and mid-back pain](/en/blog/upper-back-pain-after-car-accident).
- **Jaw and head.** Tight jaw and neck muscles can drive tension headaches; see [headaches after a car accident](/en/blog/headaches-after-car-accident).
- **Legs.** A calf or thigh that cramps after you braced hard on the brake. Usually minor, but read the warning about compartment syndrome above.

Spasms often start the next morning, not at the scene. That delay is normal and is one reason [delayed symptoms](/en/blog/delayed-symptoms-after-car-accident) catch people off guard.

## What helps

For a strained muscle without warning signs, the advice for acute back pain in MedlinePlus is a good guide for spasms in general:

- **Ice, then heat.** Use ice for the first 48 to 72 hours, then heat. For a spasm in progress, MedlinePlus suggests heat when it starts and ice once the pain eases. Protect your skin and keep sessions short.
- **Keep moving.** Reduce activity for a few days, but bed rest is not recommended; stay as active as you can. Avoid heavy lifting and twisting early on, and add light activity such as walking as you improve.
- **Gentle stretching and range of motion.** For neck pain after a traffic collision, the OPTIMa guideline recommends gentle range-of-motion exercises, taught by a professional, several times a day. Stretching hard into the spasm usually makes it fight back.
- **Fluids.** Dehydration makes cramps more likely.
- **Over-the-counter pain relievers**, used as the label says. Read [ibuprofen or Tylenol after a car accident](/en/blog/pain-relievers-after-car-accident) first, especially if you take other medicines.
- **Hands-on care and massage** can ease the tension so you can move, and work best combined with exercise; see [passive vs active care](/en/blog/passive-vs-active-care-after-car-accident).

## About muscle relaxers

Your provider may prescribe a muscle relaxer such as cyclobenzaprine for a few days or weeks. MedlinePlus notes that it is used for muscle spasms, that you should not take it for more than three weeks without talking to your doctor, and that it **may make you drowsy**. Do not drive or operate machinery until you know how it affects you, and ask your doctor about alcohol, which it can make worse. A relaxer is a short-term help to get you moving, not a treatment on its own.

## The short version

Muscle spasms after a car accident are common and usually come from strained muscles tightening to protect an injured area. Ice first, then heat, gentle movement, fluids and short-term medication when needed help most people improve within days to weeks. But a spasm can also be the body guarding a nerve, disc or bone injury, so know the warning signs, and get evaluated if it is not improving within a week. Keep a simple log of your symptoms; it helps your treatment and your claim. See [how to document your injuries for an insurance claim](/en/blog/how-to-document-injuries-insurance-claim).
