You had back pain on and off for years. It was manageable: some stiff mornings, nothing that kept you from working. Since the crash, it is different. The pain is constant, it goes down your leg and you cannot sit through a full shift.

Then the adjuster calls and says your MRI shows "degeneration," so your pain must be from before. **Having a condition before the accident does not mean the accident did not hurt you.** A crash can take a problem that was quiet or manageable and make it worse. What matters is what changed, and whether that change is documented.

## Why "degeneration" on your MRI is not the whole story

As we age, the discs and joints of the spine change. That is normal, and much of it causes no pain at all. A systematic review published in the *American Journal of Neuroradiology* looked at imaging of 3,110 people **with no back pain**:

The authors concluded that many of these findings are likely part of normal aging and must be interpreted together with the patient's symptoms. In other words, a scan alone cannot tell when a finding appeared or whether it hurts. **Your symptoms, your exam and how they changed after the crash** fill in the rest. Read more in [X-ray, MRI or CT after a car accident](/en/blog/xray-mri-ct-after-car-accident).

## How a crash can make an old problem worse

- **A quiet condition becomes painful.** Many people have disc or joint changes for years without symptoms, until a strong force like a crash irritates them.
- **A managed condition gets worse.** Pain that came and went becomes constant, spreads to new areas or starts causing tingling or weakness.
- **A new injury on top of the old one.** A disc that was already worn can herniate, or a prior injury can heal more slowly.

See also [herniated disc and sciatica after a car accident](/en/blog/herniated-disc-sciatica-after-car-accident) and [back pain after a car accident](/en/blog/back-pain-after-car-accident).

## What usually matters in the claim

In general, the person who caused the crash is not responsible for a condition you already had, but may be responsible for the extent to which the crash made it worse. That is why insurers focus on your history, and why the "before and after" comparison is so important. How this applies to your case depends on the facts, so talk with an attorney about the legal side.

In Utah, your PIP coverage pays the first medical bills after a crash regardless of who caused it. Read more in [who pays medical bills after a car accident in Utah](/en/blog/who-pays-medical-bills-car-accident-utah).

<div class="pa-check">
  <h4>How to document what changed</h4>
  <ul>
    <li>Tell your provider about every prior injury, treatment and flare-up, with approximate dates.</li>
    <li>Describe specifically what you could do before and cannot do now: work hours, lifting, sleep, exercise, driving.</li>
    <li>Point out what is new: pain in a different place, pain that travels down an arm or leg, tingling, weakness.</li>
    <li>Gather prior records and imaging if you have them, so they can be compared with the new ones.</li>
    <li>Keep a simple symptom journal and go to every appointment.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

Gaps in treatment are often used to argue that the pain is "just your old problem." Read [how missed appointments hurt injury claims](/en/blog/gaps-in-treatment-hurt-your-claim) and [how to document your injuries for an insurance claim](/en/blog/how-to-document-injuries-insurance-claim).

## When the adjuster brings up your history

You do not have to argue about your MRI on the phone. Be honest, do not guess or minimize, and do not agree with statements like "so this is the same pain you always had." Read [what to say to the insurance adjuster](/en/blog/what-to-say-insurance-adjuster) before that call.

## The short version

A prior condition does not cancel a new injury. Degenerative changes are very common in people with no pain, so a scan alone does not explain your symptoms. Tell your providers about your history, describe clearly what changed after the crash, keep your appointments and let your records show the difference.
