In a crash your arms do not stay still. The elbow can slam into the door or the window in a side impact, hit the center console, or lock straight as you brace against the dashboard or the steering wheel. A day later it is swollen, it hurts to bend, and turning a doorknob suddenly feels like a lot.

Many elbow injuries are bruises that settle in days. **But a few common fractures can look like "just a bruise" at first, and the elbow stiffens quickly when it is not moved, so the sooner you know what you are dealing with, the better it recovers.**

## How a crash hurts the elbow

The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS) describes two main ways the elbow breaks. One is a **direct blow**: falling straight onto the elbow or being hit by something hard, and OrthoInfo specifically lists a car door in a vehicle collision. The other is force that **travels up the forearm** when you land on an outstretched hand, which can break the top of the forearm bones or dislocate the elbow. Bracing a straight arm against the dashboard or the wheel puts the elbow in a similar position.

The elbow is also not always where the pain starts. Pain that runs down the arm with tingling can come from the neck or the shoulder: read [numbness or tingling in your arms or hands](/en/blog/numbness-tingling-arms-hands-after-car-accident) and [shoulder pain after a car accident](/en/blog/shoulder-pain-after-car-accident).

## Radial head fractures: the one that hides

The radial head is the top of the radius, the forearm bone on the thumb side. OrthoInfo says these fractures are common, about 20% of acute elbow injuries, and more common in women and in people aged 30 to 40.

The tricky part: a small crack may not show on the first X-ray and can become visible only a few weeks later. If your elbow hurts on the outside and you cannot fully turn your palm up, say so, even if the first film was "normal." For the simplest fractures, OrthoInfo notes a sling or splint is usually not needed beyond the first few days, if at all, and movement is increased gradually. More displaced fractures may need screws, a plate, or removing or replacing the broken piece. Even the simplest radial head fracture can leave some loss of movement, which is why therapy matters. For how imaging works, see [X-ray, CT or MRI after a car accident](/en/blog/xray-mri-ct-after-car-accident).

## Olecranon fractures: the point of the elbow

The olecranon sits right under the skin with little padding, so a direct hit can break it. OrthoInfo describes sudden, intense pain that can keep you from moving the elbow, swelling, bruising, tenderness, numbness in one or more fingers and a feeling that the joint might "pop out."

If the pieces stay in place, you typically wear a splint for about 2 weeks before switching to a brace. Displaced fractures in healthy adults, unstable elbows and open fractures (bone through the skin) usually need surgery, open fractures generally within 24 hours of diagnosis. Pain usually lasts a few days to a couple of weeks; most people are back to normal activities in about 4 months, although full healing can take more than a year. Stiffness is common, and the elbow often ends up a little stiffer than before.

## Elbow bursitis: the swelling at the tip

A sac of fluid called the bursa lets the skin glide over the point of the elbow. OrthoInfo explains that a hard blow to the tip of the elbow can make it produce extra fluid and swell. Treatment is usually an elbow pad, avoiding pressure on the elbow and an anti-inflammatory such as ibuprofen (read [ibuprofen or Tylenol after a car accident](/en/blog/pain-relievers-after-car-accident) first). If it does not improve after 3 to 6 weeks, a steroid injection may be considered.

A scrape or cut over the elbow can let bacteria into the bursa. **If the skin turns red and warm, get it checked promptly:** an infected bursa is usually drained with a needle and treated with antibiotics, and untreated infection can spread.

## Stiffness is the real enemy

The elbow loses motion fast when it is held still. After the initial protection your provider recommends, the goal is to get it moving again, gradually and under guidance: bending, straightening and turning the forearm, then strengthening the arm and grip. Physical or occupational therapy helps restore motion; see [your first six weeks of rehab after a crash](/en/blog/first-six-weeks-physical-therapy-after-crash) and [heat, ultrasound or exercise: what the evidence says](/en/blog/passive-vs-active-care-after-car-accident).

## The short version

Elbow pain after a crash is often a bruise, but the elbow is also where some easy-to-miss fractures happen: the radial head on the outside, and the olecranon at the point. Pain turning your palm, swelling with finger numbness, or an elbow you cannot move needs an evaluation, and an open wound or deformity needs the ER. Whatever the injury, getting the elbow moving again at the right time is what protects your range of motion.
