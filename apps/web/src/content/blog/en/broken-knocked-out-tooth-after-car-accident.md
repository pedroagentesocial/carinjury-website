Your face hit the steering wheel or the airbag, and now there is blood in your mouth and a front tooth is loose, chipped or missing. In the middle of everything else at the crash scene, a tooth can seem like the least of your worries.

But a knocked-out tooth is one of the few crash injuries where **the next half hour can decide whether it can be saved.** Here is what to do, and what to watch for in the days after.

## If a permanent tooth is knocked out

<div class="pa-timeline">
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Right away</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Find it and pick it up by the crown</p>
    <p>Hold the tooth by the chewing part (the crown), never by the root. If it is dirty, rinse it gently with water only. Do not use soap, do not scrub it, do not dry it and do not wrap it in a tissue or cloth.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">If you can</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Put it back in the socket</p>
    <p>The American Association of Endodontists (AAE) says to try to put it back right away: push it in gently by the crown, or set it over the socket and close your mouth slowly, then hold it in place with your fingers or a gentle bite.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">If you cannot</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Keep it moist</p>
    <p>Carry it in milk, in your mouth next to your cheek, or in a tooth preservation kit. Do not use plain tap water: the cells on the root cannot tolerate it for long.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Within 30 minutes</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Get to a dentist or endodontist</p>
    <p>Bring the tooth. The sooner, the better the chance of saving it, but do not give up if more time has passed: a tooth can sometimes be saved even after an hour or more outside the mouth.</p>
  </div>
</div>

**Baby teeth are different.** MedlinePlus notes that baby teeth are not put back in. Still have a dentist check your child, and read [children after a car accident](/en/blog/children-after-car-accident).

Do not put aspirin or pain ointments directly on the gums or the tooth. For pain relief options, read [ibuprofen or Tylenol after a car accident](/en/blog/pain-relievers-after-car-accident).

## Cracks can show up later

Not every dental injury is obvious on day one. The AAE describes cracked teeth as causing **erratic pain when chewing**, often when you release the bite, and sudden pain with very hot or cold food, and notes that pain can come and go, making it hard to find which tooth is the problem. If chewing starts to hurt days or weeks after a crash, tell your dentist about the accident.

Jaw pain, clicking or trouble opening your mouth may come from the jaw joint rather than the teeth: see [jaw pain after a car accident](/en/blog/jaw-pain-tmj-after-car-accident). And if the airbag hit your face, read [airbag injuries after a car accident](/en/blog/airbag-injuries-after-car-accident).

## Who pays for dental care after a crash in Utah

Utah personal injury protection (PIP) pays the reasonable value of necessary medical, X-ray, **dental** and rehabilitation services, among others, up to the minimum required coverage of at least $3,000 per person. Dental work can use up that amount quickly, so keep every receipt and treatment plan. Learn how the rest of your bills get paid in [who pays my medical bills after a car accident in Utah?](/en/blog/who-pays-medical-bills-car-accident-utah).

## The short version

A knocked-out permanent tooth is a race against the clock: hold it by the crown, rinse it with water only, put it back in the socket if you can or carry it in milk or inside your cheek, and see a dentist within 30 minutes, though a tooth can sometimes be saved even after an hour. Baby teeth are not put back. If your bite feels wrong, the jaw may be broken and you need emergency care. Cracks can hurt days later, and in Utah, PIP covers necessary dental services along with your other medical care.
