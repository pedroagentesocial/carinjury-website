You were on your way to a job site, making a delivery or running an errand your boss asked for, and another driver ran the light. Now you have two questions nobody answered at the scene: *Is this a work injury or a car accident? And who pays?*

In Utah, it can be both. **If the crash happened while you were working, workers' compensation will usually be involved, and the other driver can still be responsible.** The order in which the coverages pay, and the notices you have to give, are different from a regular crash.

## Does the crash count as work-related?

Workers' compensation covers injuries "arising out of and in the course of" your employment. For crashes, the question is what you were doing at the time:

<div class="pa-table-wrap">
  <table>
    <thead>
      <tr><th>Situation</th><th>Usually</th><th>Why</th></tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Driving is part of your job (deliveries, sales calls, driving between job sites)</td><td>Work-related</td><td>You were doing your job when it happened</td></tr>
      <tr><td>An errand your employer asked you to run</td><td>Often work-related</td><td>The trip was for your employer's benefit</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Your normal commute to and from work</td><td>Usually not covered</td><td>Utah courts apply the "going and coming" rule</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Commuting in a vehicle your employer provides and controls</td><td>It depends</td><td>Courts weigh the employer's benefit and control over the vehicle</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

The Utah Supreme Court has explained that accidents while going to and from work are generally not compensable, with exceptions, such as when the vehicle is an instrumentality of the employer's business. These cases depend heavily on the facts, so if you are not sure, report it to your employer anyway and ask an attorney.

If you drive for Uber, Lyft or a delivery app, you may be classified as an independent contractor, which changes the picture. Read [Uber or Lyft accident in Utah](/en/blog/uber-lyft-accident-utah-who-pays).

## Who pays first: workers' comp or PIP?

In a regular Utah crash, your own PIP pays your first medical bills. When the crash is work-related, Utah law says PIP benefits are **reduced by any workers' compensation benefits** you receive or are entitled to receive for the same accident. In practice, workers' comp usually carries the medical bills and part of the lost wages, and PIP fills in only what is left.

## You can still claim against the other driver

Workers' comp does not take away your claim against a driver who is not your employer or a coworker. Utah law allows you to receive workers' compensation **and** bring an action for damages against the third party who caused the crash. Two things to know:

- **Your employer's insurer gets paid back.** If you recover money from the other driver, the workers' comp carrier is reimbursed for what it paid, minus its proportionate share of the costs and attorney fees of the case.
- **You have to give notice.** Before suing the other driver, you must give written notice to the workers' comp carrier so it can take part.

This is one of the situations where coordinating three coverages — workers' comp, PIP and the at-fault driver's insurance — really benefits from an attorney.

## The short version

If you were working when the crash happened, workers' compensation will usually pay first and PIP is reduced by what workers' comp pays. Report the injury to your employer right away — the legal deadline is 180 days, but waiting hurts your case. Your normal commute usually does not count. And you can still claim against the driver who caused the crash, although the workers' comp insurer will be reimbursed from that recovery.
