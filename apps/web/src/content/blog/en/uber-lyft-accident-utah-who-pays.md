You were in the back seat of an Uber. Or you were stopped at a light and a car with a Lyft sticker rear-ended you. Now your neck hurts, and nobody can tell you whose insurance is supposed to pay.

The short answer: **it depends on what the driver's app showed at the exact moment of the crash.** Utah law splits every rideshare driver's shift into phases, and each phase carries different insurance.

## The three phases Utah law defines

Utah Code section 13-51-108 requires the rideshare company (Uber, Lyft) or the driver to carry **primary** insurance — the policy that pays first — while the driver is working on the platform. But the amount changes by phase:

<div class="pa-timeline">
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Phase 0 · App off</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Just a private driver</p>
    <p>If the driver was not logged in to the app, they are not working for the platform. Only their personal auto policy responds, as in any other crash.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Phase 1 · App on, no ride</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Waiting period</p>
    <p>The driver is available but has not accepted a ride yet. The law requires at least $50,000 per person, $100,000 per occurrence and $30,000 for property damage, plus PIP and uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage where the law requires it.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Phase 2 · Ride accepted</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">Prearranged ride</p>
    <p>From the moment the driver accepts your request, while they drive to pick you up, until you get out. Here the minimum jumps to <strong>$1,000,000 in liability coverage per occurrence</strong>, plus PIP and uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="pa-note pa-note-alert">
  <span class="pa-note-icon">⚠️</span>
  <div>
    <p><strong>The Phase 1 gap.</strong> The same law states that a personal auto policy is not required to cover a driver while they are providing rideshare services, and many personal policies exclude that use. That is why limits in the waiting phase are far lower than during a ride — and why it is where insurers argue the most.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Who pays in your situation

      <tr><td>A rideshare driver hit you</td><td>Your own PIP, as in any Utah crash</td><td>A claim against the driver's insurance; if the app was on, the coverage for that phase applies</td></tr>
      <tr><td>You were a pedestrian or cyclist</td><td>PIP, if you or someone in your household has auto insurance</td><td>A claim against the insurance for the driver's phase</td></tr>
      <tr><td>You were the rideshare driver</td><td>Depends on your personal policy and the one the platform keeps for that phase</td><td>A claim against the other driver if they were at fault</td></tr>

Utah is a "no-fault" state for initial injuries: your first medical bills come from **PIP**, no matter who caused the crash. Once your reasonable medical expenses exceed $3,000 — or the injury is serious under the law — you can also claim against the at-fault driver. We walk through it in [who pays your medical bills after a car accident in Utah](/en/blog/who-pays-medical-bills-car-accident-utah).

<div class="pa-check">
  <h4>What to do after a rideshare crash</h4>
  <ul>
    <li>Screenshot the trip in your app: time, route, driver name and plate.</li>
    <li>Report the crash inside the app — that records which phase the ride was in.</li>
    <li>Get the police report and write down the case number.</li>
    <li>Photograph the vehicles, the scene and any visible injuries.</li>
    <li>Collect insurance details from every driver involved, not just the rideshare driver.</li>
    <li>Get evaluated within 24–72 hours even if you feel fine: many symptoms show up late.</li>
    <li>Do not give recorded statements or sign anything with the platform's insurer before you know your diagnosis.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

## Why the phase matters so much

Two identical crashes can end with completely different coverage. If the driver had a passenger, there is up to a million dollars in liability coverage. If they were waiting for a ride, the minimum is $50,000 per person. If the app was off, they may only carry their personal policy minimums.

That is why proof of what the app showed — your trip screenshot, the platform's record — is one of the most valuable pieces of your case. And it is why waiting is a bad idea: the clock on protecting your claim starts the day of the crash. See [Utah car accident claim deadlines](/en/blog/utah-car-accident-claim-deadlines).

## The short version

In Utah, Uber and Lyft insurance is not "always on." It depends on the phase: app off, waiting for a ride, or ride in progress. Your first medical bills come from PIP, document the ride's phase from the first minute, and get evaluated early.
