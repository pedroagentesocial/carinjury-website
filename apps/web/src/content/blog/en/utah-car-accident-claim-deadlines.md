"I have four years, so there's no rush."

That sentence has cost more Utah accident claims than almost anything else we hear at the clinic. The four-year figure is real — it just answers a different question than the one people think they are asking.

<div class="pa-stats">
  <div class="pa-stat"><span class="pa-stat-num">4 years</span><span class="pa-stat-label">To file a personal injury lawsuit (Utah Code § 78B-2-307)</span></div>
  <div class="pa-stat"><span class="pa-stat-num">3 years</span><span class="pa-stat-label">To sue for property damage to your vehicle</span></div>
  <div class="pa-stat"><span class="pa-stat-num">2 years</span><span class="pa-stat-label">To file a wrongful death claim (§ 78B-2-304)</span></div>
  <div class="pa-stat"><span class="pa-stat-num">1 year</span><span class="pa-stat-label">Notice of claim if a government vehicle was involved</span></div>

<div class="pa-note pa-note-alert">
  <span class="pa-note-icon">⏱️</span>
  <div>
    <p><strong>The one-year trap.</strong> If the other vehicle was a city bus, a UTA vehicle, a police car, a school district van or any government-owned vehicle — or if a road defect contributed — you are under the Governmental Immunity Act of Utah, and a written <strong>notice of claim within one year</strong> is required. Miss it and the four-year statute is irrelevant: the claim is gone.</p>
  </div>

## The deadline nobody writes down

Here is the part that catches people. The statute of limitations is when a *court* stops accepting your case. It is not when an *insurer* stops taking you seriously — and that happens far sooner.

<div class="pa-timeline">
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Days 1–3</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">The causation window</p>
    <p>Get evaluated. A first medical visit within 72 hours is the single strongest link between the crash and your injury. After two or three weeks, that link is something you have to argue instead of something you can show.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">First 1–2 weeks</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">The evidence window</p>
    <p>Vehicles get repaired or sold for scrap. Dashcam files overwrite. Business security footage is deleted on a 7–30 day loop. Witnesses forget details and change phone numbers.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">First 30 days</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">The quick-offer window</p>
    <p>Adjusters often present an early settlement while injuries are still undiagnosed. Signing a release here permanently closes the claim — even if an MRI finds a herniated disc a month later.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Months 1–6</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">The treatment record window</p>
    <p>Consistent, documented care builds the record. Long gaps in this period do more damage to a claim than almost anything else.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Years 1–4</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">The legal window</p>
    <p>Negotiation, and if necessary, filing suit. By this point the outcome has largely been decided by what was documented in the first six months.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Two rules that change the math

**PIP first, always.** Utah is a no-fault state, so your own Personal Injury Protection pays the first medical bills regardless of fault, and you must clear the $3,000 medical threshold (or have a permanent injury) before you can claim pain and suffering from the at-fault driver. We break this down in [who pays my medical bills after a car accident in Utah](/en/blog/who-pays-medical-bills-car-accident-utah).

**Comparative fault.** Utah reduces your recovery by your share of the blame, and bars it entirely if you are found 50% or more at fault. What you say to an adjuster in week one can move that percentage — which is why an unprepared recorded statement is so expensive.

<div class="pa-check">
  <h4>Do these things this month, not in year three</h4>
  <ul>
    <li>Open your PIP claim and get the claim number.</li>
    <li>Get a documented medical evaluation, even for "minor" symptoms.</li>
    <li>Request the official crash report and save it as a PDF.</li>
    <li>Photograph both vehicles, the scene and any visible injury before repairs.</li>
    <li>Ask nearby businesses to preserve security footage — in writing, immediately.</li>
    <li>Write down witness names and phone numbers while they are still reachable.</li>
    <li>If a government vehicle was involved, talk to a lawyer about the one-year notice now.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

## The short version

Four years is the outer wall, not the schedule. The decisions that determine what your claim is worth happen in the first two to four weeks — the medical record, the physical evidence and what you did or did not say to an adjuster. Treat those weeks as the real deadline.
