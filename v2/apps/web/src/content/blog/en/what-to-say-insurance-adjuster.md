The call usually comes within 48 hours, and it is friendly. The adjuster is sympathetic, says they just need a few details to get your claim moving, and asks if it is all right to record.

Here is the part that matters: the person calling is doing a job, and the job is to resolve the claim for as little as possible. That is not a conspiracy — it is the role. What you say in that first conversation gets quoted back to you for the next two years.

## First, know which insurer you are talking to

This distinction decides everything else, and almost nobody makes it on the phone.

<div class="pa-compare">
  <div class="pa-col pa-col-good">
    <h4>Your own insurer (PIP claim)</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>You have a contract with them and a duty to cooperate</li>
      <li>Give the basic facts needed to open your PIP claim</li>
      <li>Provide the claim number to every provider you see</li>
      <li>Still stick to facts — cooperation is not speculation</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-col pa-col-bad">
    <h4>The other driver's insurer</h4>
    <ul>
      <li>You have no contract with them and no duty to be recorded</li>
      <li>You are not legally required to give a recorded statement in any US state</li>
      <li>Their job is to limit what they pay you</li>
      <li>It is entirely reasonable to say you will respond in writing</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

## The six sentences that cost the most

<div class="pa-table-wrap">
  <table>
    <thead>
      <tr><th>What people say</th><th>How it gets used</th><th>Say this instead</th></tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>"I'm fine."</td><td>Quoted as evidence there was no injury, even months later</td><td>"I'm being evaluated and I don't have a diagnosis yet."</td></tr>
      <tr><td>"I'm sorry."</td><td>Treated as an admission of fault</td><td>Nothing. Courtesy is not the same as accepting blame.</td></tr>
      <tr><td>"He was going about 45."</td><td>Contradicted by physical evidence, then framed as unreliable testimony</td><td>"I'm not going to guess at speeds or distances."</td></tr>
      <tr><td>"I didn't see them."</td><td>Reframed as inattention on your part</td><td>Describe only what you observed, in order.</td></tr>
      <tr><td>"My back has bothered me for years."</td><td>Used to attribute everything to a pre-existing condition</td><td>Let the medical record address history. Do not summarise it from memory.</td></tr>
      <tr><td>"Sure, you can record it."</td><td>Locks in a version of events before your injuries are diagnosed</td><td>"I'd prefer to provide information in writing."</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

  <span class="pa-note-icon">🎙️</span>
  <div>
    <p><strong>Why recorded statements are requested so early.</strong> The purpose is to fix your account in place before you have a diagnosis, before you have seen the police report, and before you have spoken to a lawyer. Any later medical finding that does not match your day-two description becomes an "inconsistency" — even though it is simply the normal course of a delayed-onset injury.</p>

## A script you can read out loud

You do not have to improvise. Keep this by the phone:

> "Thank you for calling. I'm happy to confirm the date, time and location of the accident, my vehicle and my insurance information. I'm not going to give a recorded statement, and I'm not going to estimate speeds, distances or my injuries — I'm still under medical evaluation. Please send any further questions in writing and I'll respond."

Then confirm only:

<div class="pa-check">
  <h4>Safe to confirm</h4>
  <ul>
    <li>Your name, address and phone number</li>
    <li>The date, approximate time and location of the crash</li>
    <li>The vehicle you were driving and your insurance information</li>
    <li>The police report number, if one was taken</li>
    <li>That you are receiving medical care — without listing diagnoses you do not yet have</li>
  </ul>
</div>

## About that early offer

An offer within the first few weeks is not generosity; it is arithmetic. It arrives while your injuries are undiagnosed, which is exactly when your claim looks cheapest.

<div class="pa-timeline">
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Week 1–3</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">The offer arrives</p>
    <p>Often framed as covering "your inconvenience" or the ER visit. Almost always presented as time-limited.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">The signature</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">A release, not a receipt</p>
    <p>Accepting means signing a release of all claims. That is final: if an MRI six weeks later shows a herniated disc, there is no legal recourse.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="pa-step">
    <span class="pa-step-when">Weeks 4–12</span>
    <p class="pa-step-title">When the real picture appears</p>
    <p>Delayed-onset injuries, imaging findings and the actual cost of rehabilitation become clear — after the window to claim them has closed.</p>
  </div>
</div>

The honest rule: do not settle an injury claim before you know the diagnosis, the treatment plan and the prognosis. In Utah there is also a threshold to consider — you generally need to exceed $3,000 in medical expenses, or have a permanent injury, before you can claim pain and suffering from the at-fault driver, which is covered in [who pays my medical bills after a car accident in Utah](/en/blog/who-pays-medical-bills-car-accident-utah).

## Two more things that end up in the file

**Medical authorizations.** A broad, open-ended release lets the insurer pull your entire medical history looking for anything pre-existing. Limit authorizations to records related to this crash, and have your attorney review them.

**Social media.** A photo of you at a family barbecue becomes "the claimant was observed lifting a cooler". You do not need to disappear online, but assume anything public will be read in the least charitable way possible.

## The short version

Be polite, be brief, and be factual. Confirm identity and logistics, decline the recording, refuse to guess, and do not describe injuries you have not been diagnosed with yet. Every one of those choices is legal, reasonable, and worth more than anything you could say to be helpful.
